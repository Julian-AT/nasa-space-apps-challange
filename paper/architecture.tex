\section{Model architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

\input{figures/encoder-architecture}

The encoder (Figure~\ref{fig:encoder}) asks whether a learned representation of elevation,
trained on all three bodies and supervised by the fingerprint, can reproduce the fingerprint on
held out regions, carry morphology that the fingerprint lacks, and give cross body neighbours that
agree with it. It is a second opinion beside the fingerprint, labelled Predicted wherever it
appears, and it enters no ranking unless the promotion gate of Section~\ref{sec:results} passes.

\paragraph{Input.} A window is resampled to \ConfigInputPx{} by \ConfigInputPx{} pixels at
\ConfigInputSpacing\,m (Section~\ref{sec:data}) and a best fit plane is removed. The three channels
are $\operatorname{asinh}(z / s)$ with $s = 50$\,m, which compresses large relief while staying
linear near zero; the slope magnitude divided by 45 degrees; and the validity mask. The same code,
in Python and in the browser, feeds training and the live probe.

\paragraph{Backbone and heads.} The backbone is a ResNet-18 \citep{he2015} at half width, with
stages of 32, 64, 128 and 256 channels, batch normalisation \citep{ioffe2015} and a three by three stem without max pooling for the small input. The specified stem stride is one; a stride of two, which quarters the compute and leaves the parameter count unchanged, is allowed as a recorded deviation for training on CPUs. Global average pooling gives the embedding $h \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$, which is
L2 normalised for retrieval. A three layer projector of width 2048 maps $h$ to $z$ for the VICReg
loss. A two layer physics head predicts the eleven standardised physics targets: $\ln \nu$ of
relief and texture at the five lags and the relief Hurst exponent. A two layer body head predicts
which of the three bodies a window came from, behind a gradient reversal layer \citep{ganin2015}.

\paragraph{Objective.} For two augmented views of one window with projections $z, z'$ over a
batch of $n$ windows and dimension $d$, the VICReg loss \citep{bardes2021} is
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{V}} &= \lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}\, \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i} \lVert z_i - z'_i \rVert_2^2
  + \lambda_{\mathrm{var}}\,\bigl(v(z) + v(z')\bigr) + \lambda_{\mathrm{cov}}\,\bigl(c(z) + c(z')\bigr),
  \label{eq:vicreg}\\
  v(z) &= \frac{1}{d} \sum_{j=1}^{d} \max\bigl(0,\, 1 - \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(z_{\cdot j}) + \epsilon}\bigr),
  \qquad
  c(z) = \frac{1}{d} \sum_{j \neq k} \operatorname{Cov}(z)_{jk}^2,
  \nonumber
\end{align}
with coefficients $\lambda_{\mathrm{inv}} = \lambda_{\mathrm{var}} = 25$ and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{cov}} = 1$. The physics loss is a masked mean squared error over the targets $y$
that exist for a window, for both views,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}} = \frac{\sum_{k} m_k\, \bigl(\hat y_k - y_k\bigr)^2}{\sum_k m_k},
  \label{eq:phys}
\end{equation}
so a lag without a supported value contributes nothing instead of a zero target. The body loss is
the cross entropy of the body head. Gradient reversal multiplies its gradient by $-\lambda$ on the
way into the backbone, so the total objective
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{V}} + \alpha\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{body}},
  \qquad \lambda(p) = \lambda_{\max} \Bigl(\frac{2}{1 + e^{-10 p}} - 1\Bigr),
  \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
trains the head to recognise the body while the backbone is pushed not to encode it. Here $p$ is
the fraction of training completed, $\alpha = \ConfigAlphaPhys$, and $\lambda_{\max}$ is swept over
\ConfigLambdas.

\paragraph{Views.} The two views of a window differ by one of the eight rotations and reflections,
Gaussian height noise of 1 m mapped through the local derivative of the asinh channel, and up to
10\% extra nodata holes. Amplitude and scale changes are excluded, because they change the physics
targets that the views must share. Each batch draws a third of its windows from each body.
