\section{Why learn a terrain embedding}
\label{sec:embedding}

The fingerprint of Section~\ref{sec:fingerprint} is exact, auditable and cheap, but it is a second
order statistic. Two surfaces with the same structure function can differ in what produces it: a
field of overlapping craters and a set of parallel ridges may share $\nu(L)$ at every lag while
looking nothing alike. A learned embedding can in principle carry such morphology. It also brings
risks that the fingerprint does not have: it can encode the instrument, the processing or the body
instead of the terrain, and it can find neighbours for reasons nobody can inspect. This section
states what each representation encodes, so that the tests of Section~\ref{sec:results} ask the
right questions of it.

Table~\ref{tab:representations} compares four representations. The fingerprint encodes exactly
the relief and texture structure function and the Hurst exponent at stated lags, and it is
invariant to rotation by construction of the isotropic summary. Two frozen image models serve as
baselines: a ResNet-18 trained on ImageNet photographs \citep{he2015} and a Vision Transformer \citep{dosovitskiy2020} trained by DINO self distillation without labels \citep{caron2021}. Neither has seen
elevation data, so they test whether generic visual features already carry the fingerprint. The
encoder of Section~\ref{sec:architecture} learns from elevation of the three bodies with an explicit physics target and an explicit penalty on body information.

Related learned models make different choices. LunarFM learns a joint embedding of many lunar
instruments within a latitude band that excludes the poles \citep{gironamata2026}; topo2vec learns elevation embeddings with a scale based self supervision \citep{kavitzky2021}; GEAR retrieves analogs between two terrestrial regions and releases an expert annotated similarity dataset \citep{liu2026}; and the Qaidam study
learns a similarity between Earth and Mars images \citep{meng2024}. The encoder studied here uses
elevation only, three bodies, a south polar lunar target and the fingerprint as supervision, and it
is to be evaluated by probes on held out spatial blocks. Whether it adds anything over the fingerprint is
an empirical question with a stated test (Section~\ref{sec:results-ml}), and until that test passes
the fingerprint alone ranks candidates.
