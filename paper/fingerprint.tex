\section{Physics fingerprint}
\label{sec:fingerprint}

The fingerprint is the deterministic description of a window that every ranking in this paper
uses. It follows the recommendation of \citet{shepard2001} to report roughness at stated measurement scales and the baseline conventions of \citet{rosenburg2011}, measured with one code path on every body.

\paragraph{Structure function.} Let $z$ be the elevation of a window on the analysis grid, with
validity mask $m$. For a lag $L$ along a direction $d$, let $P_d(L)$ be the set of cell pairs
$(a, b)$ with $b = a + L\,\hat d$ and both cells valid. The structure function is
\begin{equation}
  \nu_d(L) = \Bigl(\frac{1}{\lvert P_d(L)\rvert} \sum_{(a,b) \in P_d(L)}
  \bigl(z(b) - z(a)\bigr)^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
  \label{eq:nu}
\end{equation}
It is measured along both grid axes and both diagonals. A lag is used along a direction only if
it is a whole number of steps there to arithmetic tolerance; lags are never rounded to a nearby
offset and cells are never interpolated. On the \ConfigGridM\,m grid no diagonal offset equals a
configured lag, so the diagonals are recorded as unsupported and the isotropic value rests on the
two axes. The isotropic summary weights the supported directions $\mathcal{D}(L)$ equally,
\begin{equation}
  \nu(L) = \Bigl(\frac{1}{\lvert \mathcal{D}(L)\rvert} \sum_{d \in \mathcal{D}(L)} \nu_d(L)^2\Bigr)^{1/2},
  \label{eq:isotropic}
\end{equation}
and the per direction values are kept beside it. The configured lags are \ConfigLags\,m: at least
three analysis cells and at most a quarter of the \ConfigWindowM\,m window. A window is measured
only if at least \ConfigMinValidPct\% of its cells are valid, and an unsupported lag has no value,
never a value of zero.

\paragraph{Relief and texture.} Each window is measured twice. Relief is
Equation~\eqref{eq:nu} on the raw heights, tilt included. Texture is the same statistic on the
residual $z - (a + b x + c y)$ after the least squares plane over the valid cells is removed, with
$x$ east and $y$ north in metres from the window centre. The plane is solved exactly over the
stored values, so an exact plane leaves a texture of exactly zero rather than rounding noise. Both
variants are named in every record, because removing the plane can erase the very property being
compared.

\paragraph{Hurst exponent.} $H$ is the ordinary least squares slope of $\ln \nu(L)$ against
$\ln L$ over the supported lags,
\begin{equation}
  H = \frac{\sum_k (\ell_k - \bar\ell)(n_k - \bar n)}{\sum_k (\ell_k - \bar\ell)^2},
  \qquad \ell_k = \ln L_k,\quad n_k = \ln \nu(L_k),
  \label{eq:hurst}
\end{equation}
which equals the exponent for $\nu = c L^H$. It is fitted only with at least \ConfigMinLagsH{}
supported lags that span at least a factor of \ConfigLagSpan, and only if $\nu$ is positive at
every lag. Otherwise $H$ is unavailable with its reason; a zero is never offset to take its
logarithm. Over our five lags $H$ describes the range from a few hundred metres to about a
kilometre, the range in which \citet{rosenburg2011} report a breakover on the Moon, so
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-data-analysis} tests for a break over a wider range of lags at the scale of the whole site rather than assuming a single exponent.

\paragraph{Terrain distance.} Two windows $u$ and $w$ are compared on the lags $S_{uw}$ supported
in both, separately for relief and texture:
\begin{equation}
  D(u, w) = \Bigl(\frac{1}{\lvert S_{uw}\rvert} \sum_{L \in S_{uw}}
  \bigl(\ln \nu_u(L) - \ln \nu_w(L)\bigr)^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
  \label{eq:distance}
\end{equation}
A difference of $\ln \nu$ is a ratio of relief, so $D$ does not depend on the units of height and
weighs a factor of two the same at every lag. Relief and texture distances are never summed with
each other, and in this study no radar distance exists to sum them with
(Section~\ref{sec:results-radar}). Candidates are ordered by Pareto layers over the two distances,
with the window identifier as a deterministic tie break; no single score is formed.

\paragraph{Reference and uncertainty.} Each distance is meant to be read against two references, and Section~\ref{sec:results-retrieval} states which were computed. The target
reference set is the spread of distances between the primary target window and the other eligible
Site01 windows, which measures how much the target varies internally. The background is a fixed, seeded sample, configured at \ConfigBackgroundSample{} eligible windows from the same search universe, against
which a candidate's distance is a percentile. For the Moon the provider's \ConfigClones{} error
clones of the elevation model \citep{barker2021} are measured like the original, so each
statistic has a spread under the published height error model. The clones represent that error
model only; they say nothing about rocks below the grid or about radar. Candidates whose distances
differ by less than their combined spread are reported as tied, and the stability of a rank across
clones is a frequency, never the probability that a site is a valid analog.
